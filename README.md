# Sona Mindful Social

Website Script:

I want to build an app called Sona — a Gen Z social feed where the filtering happens before content reaches you: you can curate what gets through, tune your algorithm with dials instead of guesswork, soften or hold back sensitive posts, and share just enough with your circle. There will be a shell page, feed screen, and profile page to start

I'll build **Sona** — a Gen Z social feed where the filtering happens before content reaches you: curate what gets through, tune your algorithm with dials instead of guesswork, soften or hold back sensitive posts, and share just enough with your circle. Starting with the shell and the feed screen.




Sona is built — feed, curation, algorithm dials, circle and settings, all inside the login gate.

Feed: filters before it reaches you: held-back posts collapse to a quiet line, softened ones blur until you tap, and "Why this?" explains every verdict.

Curate: gives eight topics three settings each, plus Gentle / Balanced / Strict guard levels with live counts.

Vibe: replaces guesswork with eight dials, four starter presets and a live mix bar.

Circle:  shares only a song title and a mood dot, with quiet mode.

Settings:  holds the age modes: Sprout (the child's version, with hard blocks and a smaller feed), Bloom and Open.




The app will comply cleanly and visually confirm the shell, greeting, mood check-in, real post count and circle panel rendering with your saved data. I hit the preview limit while fixing a stuck loading state at the end, so the finished feed with posts actually held back and softened — plus the reveal action, guard-level saving and the Curate/Vibe/Circle/Settings screens — are **not automatically verified**; worth a quick click-through on your side.

There is a unique additional feature for the user that the user can toggle to feel more inclined to use the app. Small little characters called Sonas will provide the user with a digital helper. These small rounded white buddies can be customized using a wide range of skin tones, hair types/styles/colors, body types, as well as a wide variety of clothing options. Users can also customize by scribbling over their Sona if they choose to. Your sona appears whenever you might need to take a rest, or to provide you with your daily mental health check-in. If you choose to, your Sona and those of your circles can interact within a small virtual pocket world called the Zone. The Zone is a separate page, a part of the Sona shell, where helpers can sit, play, or emote freely. If you choose to allow other Sonas to interact with yours, you and your circle can play minigames with your Sonas. 




There are four available minigames coded to the style of a Google Doodle. These include Tic Tac Toe,  Rock Paper Scissors, Odds and Evens, and Dots and Boxes.

What is Mental Health?

According to the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA), mental health revolves around one’s “emotional, psychological, and social well-being.” It can determine the manner in which a person thinks or acts in a variety of situations, from small choices to working in stressful environments. Throughout recent years, conversations around the topic have grown as an effort in normalizing the help that some individuals may need to confront psychological disorders. Understanding and bringing awareness to the origins of these mental disorders and finding effective curative techniques for such can promote a deeper insight into mental health.

Main Disorders Affecting Teens:



Anxiety



Anxiety disorder is a mental condition characterized by intense worry about everyday situations. These apprehensive thoughts and beliefs can often exhibit themselves through physical symptoms, such as a rapid heart rate and breathing, as well as sweating. It is believed that anxiety originates from imbalances in the limbic system, or the emotional center of the brain. Another possible determinant for anxiety is decreased inhibitory signaling by gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) or increased glutamate excitatory neurotransmission (Martin et al. 2009). 

Furthermore, anxiety disorders have several common stressors. Experiencing stress and trauma at a young age, such as physical and emotional abuse, as well as neglect, can project into present stages of life (Watson). Additionally, being subjected to stressful daily situations, such as demanding and arduous hours at work or school, can lead to an increased risk of developing chronic anxious thoughts. Moreover, being presented with new life challenges or changes can also trigger this condition and any further fear of what might await in these new situations. 

According to the Anxiety and Depression Association of America, anxiety-related conditions are the most common mental illness in the United States, with more than 40 million adults aged eighteen or older experiencing it. Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD), which is a form of severe anxiety that interferes with day-to-day activities, affects 6.8 million adults aged eighteen or older in the United States. However, only 43.2% of this group is getting treatment. When this anxiety and stress become overbearing, physical ailments start displaying, such as increased risks of high blood pressure, heart disease, ulcers, irritable bowel syndrome, and skin problems like eczema. As anxiety has become a growing concern, we invite you to continue informing yourself and others about anxiety. Below are some resources and links listed to continue your research.

Depression



Neurological:  Depression involves alterations in brain chemistry and function. Neurotransmitters such as serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine play significant roles in mood regulation. Imbalances or dysfunction in these neurotransmitter systems can contribute to depressive symptoms. Origins/Stressors: Various factors can contribute to the development of depression, including genetics, environmental stressors, traumatic experiences, chronic illness, and substance abuse. Chronic stress, in particular, can lead to changes in brain structure and function that increase susceptibility to depression.



Eating Disorders: 



Neurological: Eating disorders such as anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, and binge eating disorder involve complex interactions between biological, psychological, and sociocultural factors. Neurobiological studies suggest that abnormalities in brain regions involved in appetite regulation, reward processing, and cognitive control may contribute to the development and maintenance of eating disorders. Origins/Stressors: Eating disorders often develop in response to a combination of genetic predisposition, psychological factors, involving low self-esteem, perfectionism, and body image dissatisfaction; environmental influences, such as societal pressure to attain thinness; and stressful life events like trauma or major life changes.



ASD




Neurological: Autism spectrum disorder (ASD), is a neurological disorder that affects the way people interact with others in the way they communicate and behave. People with ASD often have difficulty with communication and interaction with others, interests that are restricted, and behaviors that are scheduled and repetitive. These symptoms affect every part of their daily life, including work and school.  Origins/Stressors: Though ASD has no single known cause, both genetics and the environment plays a role in the cause of autism. For instance, syndromes such as Rett syndrome or fragile X syndrome are associated with autism, and genetic mutations also increase the risk of ASD. Furthermore, research is still being conducted to see whether factors such as infections, medications, or pregnancy complications may lead to autism. However, it is known that boys are four times more likely to develop autism, and families who have one child with autism are more likely to have another child with autism. Stats: About 4 in 100 boys and 1 in 100 girls have autism in the United States. Autism prevalence is also lower among white children than in any other racial group.

ADHD/ADD



Neurological: ADHD, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, causes people to have trouble paying attention, controlling impulsive behaviors, and being overly active. A child with ADHD may daydream a lot, be overly forgetful, squirm and fidget, talk too much, and take unnecessary risks. There are three types of ADHD: predominantly inattentive presentation, where the individual is unable to organize or finish tasks and pay attention to details, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive presentation, where the person fidgets and talks a lot and feels restless, as well as combined presentation, where the symptoms for both presentations are present. 

Origins/Stressors: The causes of ADHD include brain injury, exposure of environment risks, such as lead, during pregnancy, alcohol and tobacco use during pregnancy, premature delivery, and low birth weight. Usually, ADHD is diagnosed in early childhood, but ADHD can last into adulthood, and some adults have undiagnosed ADHD. 

Stats: 6 million children aged 3-17 years have been diagnosed with ADHD, the majority being 12-17 years old. Boys are more likely to be diagnosed with ADHD than girls. Black and white children are more often diagnosed with ADHD than any other race.

Combatting the ‘Finite Feed’: 

A mental health-forward replacement for toxic doomscrolling feeds requires shifting the core product psychology from variable reward maximization (keeping you hooked) to intentional consumption and closure. Sona is not meant to curate ‘addiction.’ It should be made to combat the toxic effects of the modern algorithm and feed. Much like other apps, it will include a ‘Reels’ sections, but there will be a health consious focus on how to redesign these slotmachine like posts to protect our users. 

Traditional feeds are designed to mimic slot machines (infinite scroll, unpredictable rewards). A mindful redesign replaces anxiety-inducing loops with boundaries.

The "Finite Feed" over Infinite Scroll: Instead of an endless stream, the feed is delivered in daily or semi-daily "editions" (e.g., 10–15 curated posts). Once a user finishes, the app explicitly displays a "You're All Caught Up" closure screen and locks further scrolling.

Pull-to-Refresh Alternative: Replacing the quick drag-down refresh (which triggers a dopamine spike) with a slow, intentional breathing prompt or a mandatory reflection question before new content loads.

Content Categorization Tabs: Separating content into distinct buckets (e.g., "Inspiration," "Learning," "Close Friends") rather than a single, chaotic algorithmic soup. This prevents emotional whiplash (e.g., seeing a tragedy immediately followed by a dance trend).

Key Time Thresholds and Risks

30 minutes a day: Studies cited by groups like Harvard Health suggest that keeping social media use to around 30 minutes daily provides a safer baseline for mental well-being. Sona will work to follow these guidelines and sustain limits. [1]

2 to 2.5 hours a day: Research on U.S. adults indicates that the link between social media scrolling and depression grows much stronger after crossing the 2.5-hour mark. [1]

4 or more hours a day: Heavy, unstructured scrolling or short-form video consumption is linked to reduced attention spans, lower impulse control, poorer sleep, and elevated stress. [1, 2]

2. Rewiring the Algorithmic Incentives

Current algorithms optimize strictly for "Watch Time" and "Engagement" (shares, angry comments, saves), which naturally amplifies outrage and anxiety.

Optimizing for "Time Well Spent": The algorithm prioritizes content that users explicitly rate highly after viewing, rather than content they passively stared at for a long time.

The "Cool-Down" Filter: If a topic is trending rapidly or causing high emotional volatility in comment sections, the algorithm actively dampens its distribution to prevent viral panic and doomscrolling loops.

Diversification Caps: Limiting how many consecutive videos of the same sub-genre (e.g., true crime, political commentary, body transformation) a user can see in one session to prevent them from falling down echo-chambers or trigger-holes.

3. Introducing Conscious Friction & Agency

Social apps usually eliminate all friction to keep users moving seamlessly from one piece of content to the next. Injecting mindful friction forces the brain out of autopilot mode.
